# Cover prompt — what-is-a-relational-database-postgresql

Paste blocks 1–8 into an image tool. Blocks 4–8 are the locked house style, copied verbatim from
`blog-visuals/references/cover-prompt.md` by way of part 3's prompt. Do not edit them: they are
what makes the series' covers read as one set.

A deterministic fallback already exists at `assets/cover.png`, built with the series toolchain.
It has the title in Instrument Serif at x=560, the same grid as parts 1–3, and two small grids of
tiles joined by a thin line, one row of the right grid shown twice in tangerine, over a row of
four dots. Use it on any day when the post matters more than the cover.

**Alt text for the cover:** Two grids of square tiles joined by a thin line, one row of the right grid shown twice in tangerine, four dots in a row beneath

---

```
1. CONCEPT
Two small rectangular grids of identical square tiles stand side by side, like two tables of
rows and columns: a narrow one three tiles wide and a wider one five tiles wide, both six tiles
tall. A single thin line runs between them, leaving one tile of the wider grid and arriving at
one tile of the narrower grid, like a reference from one table to the other. In the wider grid,
two neighbouring rows are identical and both are coloured: the same row stored twice. Beneath
both grids sits a short horizontal line with four small open circles evenly spaced along it,
like four stations a request passes through. The doubled coloured row is the focal element.

2. COMPOSITION
The two grids sit in the left half of the frame, slightly above the vertical centre, with a
clear gap between them that the thin connecting line crosses. The line of four circles runs
below them, about as wide as both grids together. The right half of the frame is near-empty,
holding only white-smoke, so a title overlay has room.

3. TECHNIQUE
Flat editorial technical illustration. Fine consistent line weight, flat fills with no internal
gradients, subtle paper grain over the whole frame. Tiles have softly rounded corners and no
internal detail; the circles are outlines only.

4. PALETTE
Palette, strictly limited to these five colours and their tints:
jet-black #2d3142, blue-slate #4f5d75, silver #bfc0c0,
white-smoke #f5f5f5, atomic-tangerine #eb6c36.
White-smoke dominates as the background. Jet-black and blue-slate carry
structure. Atomic-tangerine is used once, on the single focal element, and
covers less than five percent of the frame. No other hues anywhere.

5. LIGHT AND MOOD
Even, diffuse light with no dramatic shadows. Calm, precise, and technical
rather than energetic. The mood of a well-made reference book, not a
conference keynote slide.

6. FRAME
16:9 aspect ratio, 1200 by 630 pixels. Keep all meaningful content within a
5 percent margin from every edge. The composition must survive being cropped
to a square and to a 4:1 banner.

7. NEGATIVE
Do not include: any text, letters, numbers, words, labels, or captions.
No logos, watermarks, signatures, or UI chrome. No photorealism, no stock-photo
people, no 3D chrome or glossy renders, no lens flare, no drop shadows.
No colours outside the stated palette. No busy detail — large readable shapes
only, since this is viewed at thumbnail size.

8. HOUSE STYLE
House style: an editorial technical illustration for a computer-science
publication. Flat vector-like shapes with fine line work, generous negative
space, and a single point of emphasis. Restrained and geometric. It should
look hand-designed for this specific article rather than generic tech art,
and it should sit comfortably beside clean black-and-white technical diagrams
without competing with them. Composed to stay legible at 300 pixels wide.
```
