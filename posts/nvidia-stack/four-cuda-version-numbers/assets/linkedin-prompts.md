# LinkedIn image prompts — four-cuda-version-numbers

Six alternative image prompts, one per shared feed card, for the abstract editorial-illustration
route. These are not descriptions of the rendered cards in `assets/social/`; those are the
shipped assets and they carry their own text. These prompts produce wordless illustrations in a
single consistent visual world, for use when a carousel needs imagery rather than typography.

The recurring objects across all six frames: four thin stacked plates, a shallow plinth, a fine
caliper, a hovering horizontal ceiling plane, fine alignment rods, a graduated rail, and a
slotted carrier. Keeping those objects constant is what makes the six read as one sequence, and
it deliberately reuses part 1's plate-and-plinth world so the series holds together.

## 01 — One word, four plates

- Narrative beat: Four things share one name, and only the middle two are measured against each other.
- File: `linkedin-01-four-plates.png`
- Alt text: Four thin plates of different materials stand apart on a plinth, the middle two joined by a fine caliper.

### Prompt

```text
SCENE AND INTENDED USE
A 4:5 LinkedIn carousel opening frame showing one apparently single object resolving into four separately made parts.

SUBJECT
Four thin rectangular plates float in a vertical column above a shallow white-smoke plinth, separated by clear gaps. Each plate has a visibly different thickness and surface treatment. A fine caliper spans only the middle two plates, touching neither the topmost nor the lowest.

KEY DETAILS
Establish the recurring objects for the whole sequence: four thin stacked plates, a shallow plinth, a fine caliper, and a horizontal ceiling plane that will appear in later frames. Draw the caliper's two jaws seated precisely on the facing surfaces of plates two and three, so the measurement is unmistakably between them alone. Put the single atomic-tangerine accent on the caliper. Arrange the column slightly left of centre in a three-quarter orthographic view with generous space to the right.

CONSTRAINTS
4:5 portrait frame, designed for a LinkedIn carousel. Editorial technical
illustration with flat vector-like shapes, fine consistent line work, subtle
paper grain, and generous negative space. Palette strictly limited to
jet-black #2d3142, blue-slate #4f5d75, silver #bfc0c0, white-smoke #f5f5f5,
and atomic-tangerine #eb6c36. White-smoke dominates; atomic-tangerine appears
once on the focal element and covers less than five percent of the frame.
Maintain the same visual world, scale language, line weight, palette, and
recurring objects across every frame in the sequence. One idea per frame.
No text, letters, numbers, labels, captions, logos, watermarks, signatures, UI
chrome, photorealism, stock-photo people, glossy 3D, lens flare, or drop
shadows. Keep meaningful content inside a five percent safe margin and use
large shapes that remain legible at mobile-feed size.
```

```text
SCENE AND INTENDED USE
A 4:5 LinkedIn carousel frame distinguishing a limit from an inventory.

SUBJECT
A thin translucent horizontal plane hovers above the plate column, extending well past it on both sides. Nothing rests on the plane and nothing hangs from it. The plates beneath sit at their own heights, untouched by it.

KEY DETAILS
Reuse the plates and plinth from the first frame and introduce the ceiling plane as a permanent object. Draw the plane as a single ruled line with a hairline thickness, so it reads as a boundary rather than a shelf. Leave a deliberate empty gap between the topmost plate and the plane. A 4:5 LinkedIn carousel closing frame showing many identifiers and a single point of use.
```

```text
SUBJECT
```

```text
KEY DETAILS
Reuse the plates and plinth, and introduce the reader slot as the final object. Make all six tags identical in shape and differ only in thread length, so no tag looks more important than another except by where it ends up. Draw the seated tag fully inside the slot with its thread taut. Place the atomic-tangerine accent on the reader slot. Compose with generous space at the top for the carousel's closing breathing room.
```
