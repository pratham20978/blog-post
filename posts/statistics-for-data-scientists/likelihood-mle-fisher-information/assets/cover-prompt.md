# Cover prompt — likelihood-mle-fisher-information

Paste blocks 1–8 into an image tool. Blocks 4–8 are the locked house style and are
copied verbatim from `blog-visuals/references/cover-prompt.md` — do not edit them.

A deterministic fallback already exists at `assets/cover-likelihood-mle-fisher-information.png`, built in
the diagram-design skin by `_cover.py` (title in Instrument Serif; Figure 2 reduced to its argument:
Clarke's log-likelihood for the V-1 rate with the three distances from the MLE to the null value 0.85,
the horizontal gap for Wald in slate, the drop in height for the likelihood ratio in ink, and the slope at
the null for the score as the one tangerine line). It is the cover `manifest.md` lists; use this prompt
only if an illustrated cover is wanted instead, and then update the manifest's COVER row to `generated`.

The concept is the article's central picture, Buse's geometry of the three tests: the log-likelihood as
one hill, the estimate at its summit, and a value under test standing on its flank. How far that value is
from the summit can be measured along the ground, down from the summit's height, or by the steepness of
the hillside where it stands.

**Alt text for an illustrated cover:** A smooth hill in side elevation with a flag on its summit, a staff
on its flank, and a tangerine board laid along the slope at the staff.

```
1. CONCEPT
A single smooth hill seen in side elevation, like a surveyor's drawing of a
landscape, rising from a flat plain. A small survey flag stands on its
summit. Partway down the left flank stands a surveyor's staff. Three
measuring instruments join the staff to the summit: a chain stretched along
the plain between their feet, a plumb line hanging from the summit's height
down to the top of the staff, and a straight board laid flat against the
hillside where the staff stands, following its slope. The board is the
focal element.

2. COMPOSITION
The hill occupies the left half of the frame, its summit slightly left of
centre and a little above the middle. The staff stands on the left flank
about halfway down. The chain runs along the plain in the lower band, the
plumb line hangs in the open air left of the summit, and the board rests on
the flank at the staff. The right third holds only the plain's horizon line
and empty white-smoke ground.

3. TECHNIQUE
Flat editorial technical illustration in the manner of a surveyor's
elevation drawing. Fine consistent line weight, flat fills with no gradients
inside shapes, subtle paper grain over the whole frame. The hill's outline
in jet-black over a pale blue-slate fill, the flag, staff, chain and plumb
line in blue-slate and jet-black, the plain's horizon in silver. Only the
board is atomic-tangerine.

4. PALETTE
Palette, strictly limited to these five colours and their tints:
jet-black #2d3142, blue-slate #4f5d75, silver #bfc0c0,
white-smoke #f5f5f5, atomic-tangerine #eb6c36.
White-smoke dominates as the background. Jet-black and blue-slate carry
structure. Atomic-tangerine is used once, on the single focal element, and
covers less than five percent of the frame. No other hues anywhere.

5. LIGHT AND MOOD
Even, diffuse light with no dramatic shadows. Calm, precise, and technical
rather than energetic. The mood of a well-made reference book, not a
conference keynote slide.

6. FRAME
16:9 aspect ratio, 1200 by 630 pixels. Keep all meaningful content within a
5 percent margin from every edge. The composition must survive being cropped
to a square and to a 4:1 banner.

7. NEGATIVE
Do not include: any text, letters, numbers, words, labels, or captions.
No logos, watermarks, signatures, or UI chrome. No photorealism, no stock-photo
people, no 3D chrome or glossy renders, no lens flare, no drop shadows.
No colours outside the stated palette. No busy detail — large readable shapes
only, since this is viewed at thumbnail size.

8. HOUSE STYLE
House style: an editorial technical illustration for a computer-science
publication. Flat vector-like shapes with fine line work, generous negative
space, and a single point of emphasis. Restrained and geometric. It should
look hand-designed for this specific article rather than generic tech art,
and it should sit comfortably beside clean black-and-white technical diagrams
without competing with them. Composed to stay legible at 300 pixels wide.
```
