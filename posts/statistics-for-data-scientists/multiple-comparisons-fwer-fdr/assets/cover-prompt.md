# Cover prompt — multiple-comparisons-fwer-fdr

Paste the whole prompt below into the image tool; blocks 4–8 are the house blocks, copied verbatim. Save the result as `assets/cover.png` (1200 × 630).

```text
1. Concept: thousands of tests, each one a chance to be fooled. A long row of identical round sieves, each catching small grey beads that fall from above; almost every bead stays caught, but here and there one slips through by chance, and in a single sieve one bead that slips through is orange, the one real find among the lucky noise.

2. Composition: the sieves stand in one long row that recedes from the lower left toward the upper middle in gentle isometric perspective, so the row reads as many without counting. Grey beads fall in faint vertical streams above each sieve; a few grey beads lie below the row where they slipped through. The orange bead sits just below one sieve about a third of the way along the row. Leave the right third and the lower right empty for a title overlay.

3. Technique: precise technical illustration with isometric line work, fine 1-pixel strokes for the sieve meshes and rims, flat fills, and a very light paper grain.

4. Palette, strictly limited to these five colours and their tints: jet-black #2d3142, blue-slate #4f5d75, silver #bfc0c0, white-smoke #f5f5f5, atomic-tangerine #eb6c36. White-smoke dominates as the background. Jet-black and blue-slate carry structure. Atomic-tangerine is used once, on the single focal element, and covers less than five percent of the frame. No other hues anywhere.

5. Even, diffuse light with no dramatic shadows. Calm, precise, and technical rather than energetic. The mood of a well-made reference book, not a conference keynote slide.

6. 16:9 aspect ratio, 1200 by 630 pixels. Keep all meaningful content within a 5 percent margin from every edge. The composition must survive being cropped to a square and to a 4:1 banner.

7. Do not include: any text, letters, numbers, words, labels, or captions. No logos, watermarks, signatures, or UI chrome. No photorealism, no stock-photo people, no 3D chrome or glossy renders, no lens flare, no drop shadows. No colours outside the stated palette. No busy detail — large readable shapes only, since this is viewed at thumbnail size.

8. House style: an editorial technical illustration for a computer-science publication. Flat vector-like shapes with fine line work, generous negative space, and a single point of emphasis. Restrained and geometric. It should look hand-designed for this specific article rather than generic tech art, and it should sit comfortably beside clean black-and-white technical diagrams without competing with them. Composed to stay legible at 300 pixels wide.
```
