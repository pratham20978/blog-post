# Cover prompt — linear-regression-inference

Paste blocks 1–8 into an image tool. Blocks 4–8 are the locked house style and are
copied verbatim from `blog-visuals/references/cover-prompt.md` — do not edit them.

A deterministic fallback already exists at `assets/cover-linear-regression-inference.png`, built in the
diagram-design skin by `_cover.py` (title in Instrument Serif; Figure 3 reduced to its argument: the correct digits that
Householder QR and the normal equations keep on Longley's and Filip's regressions, against NIST's 15 certified digits,
the certified line in tangerine, the one use). It is the cover `manifest.md` lists; use this prompt only if an
illustrated cover is wanted instead, and then update the manifest's COVER row to `generated`.

The concept is the article's central picture: least squares is a projection. The fit is a point's shadow on a flat
surface, and the residual is the line dropped straight down to it.

**Alt text for an illustrated cover:** A small sphere hangs above a flat tilted plane; a thin vertical line drops from
it to its shadow on the plane.

```
1. CONCEPT
A small sphere suspended in empty space above a broad, flat, slightly tilted
plane, seen from an elevated three-quarter view. Directly beneath the sphere
its shadow sits on the plane as a crisp ellipse. A single thin straight line
drops from the sphere to the centre of its shadow, meeting the plane at a
right angle. That line is the focal element.

2. COMPOSITION
The plane spans the lower two thirds as a large parallelogram receding to the
right; the sphere floats in the upper middle; the drop line connects them a
little right of centre. A faint regular grid lies on the plane. The upper
left third is near-empty, holding only a soft gradient.

3. TECHNIQUE
Flat editorial technical illustration with fine consistent line weight,
flat fills with no internal gradients, and subtle paper grain over the
whole frame. The plane drawn as a flat tinted parallelogram with a thin
outline; the sphere as a flat disc with one flat crescent of shade; a tiny
right-angle mark where the line meets the plane.

4. PALETTE
Palette, strictly limited to these five colours and their tints:
jet-black #2d3142, blue-slate #4f5d75, silver #bfc0c0,
white-smoke #f5f5f5, atomic-tangerine #eb6c36.
White-smoke dominates as the background. Jet-black and blue-slate carry
structure. Atomic-tangerine is used once, on the single focal element, and
covers less than five percent of the frame. No other hues anywhere.

5. LIGHT AND MOOD
Even, diffuse light with no dramatic shadows. Calm, precise, and technical
rather than energetic. The mood of a well-made reference book, not a
conference keynote slide.

6. FRAME
16:9 aspect ratio, 1200 by 630 pixels. Keep all meaningful content within a
5 percent margin from every edge. The composition must survive being cropped
to a square and to a 4:1 banner.

7. NEGATIVE
Do not include: any text, letters, numbers, words, labels, or captions.
No logos, watermarks, signatures, or UI chrome. No photorealism, no stock-photo
people, no 3D chrome or glossy renders, no lens flare, no drop shadows.
No colours outside the stated palette. No busy detail — large readable shapes
only, since this is viewed at thumbnail size.

8. HOUSE STYLE
House style: an editorial technical illustration for a computer-science
publication. Flat vector-like shapes with fine line work, generous negative
space, and a single point of emphasis. Restrained and geometric. It should
look hand-designed for this specific article rather than generic tech art,
and it should sit comfortably beside clean black-and-white technical diagrams
without competing with them. Composed to stay legible at 300 pixels wide.
```
