description: Research a topic and build the source ledger
---

Use the **blog-research** skill for: $ARGUMENTS

Start with the literature sweep, before any general web search. Look for a survey
or review first, then query the scholarly databases the skill names — Google
Scholar, arXiv, Semantic Scholar, DBLP, the ACM and IEEE libraries, the open
proceedings sites, and the industrial lab publication pages. Chase citations one
hop in both directions from the two most central papers; the forward hop is what
surfaces the correction and the superseding method.

Then run the rest of the query ladder in full, including the rung on what is
contested or superseded.

Write `research/ledger.md` with one row per claim, our own words, and the
condition column filled in. Every source row carries its venue, year, identifier
and peer-review status. Record every query in `## Search log` as you run it.

When a paper will support a planned section, inspect its figures and captions.
Add only directly relevant candidates to `## Figure candidates`, with the
original figure number, section or claim served, source URL, and verified reuse
rights. Do not inventory every image in the paper.

If the topic genuinely has no research literature, write `## Literature note`
saying so and naming what was used instead. Never invent citations to reach a
count.

Report the source list and what the post can honestly claim. Do not draft prose.
